Partitioned Survival Modelling

See how survival curves divide a cohort into health states, calculate time, costs and QALYs, and expose what happens when clinical endpoints are modelled without a structural link.

Teaching data: All numerical examples, rates, costs and utilities are hypothetical. Curves are illustrative exponential functions, not fitted to a clinical trial. ToT is the time-on-treatment survival curve (time to permanent discontinuation or death, by this lab's definition). DoT is duration of treatment: the patient-level duration or its mean/median summary, not an additional independent curve.
Part one · the foundation

Start with two curves. Derive three proportions.

The survival curves are cumulative and overlapping descriptions of patients. At any time, PFS (progression-free survival) is the share alive without progression. OS (overall survival) is the share alive, whether progressed or not. Subtracting PFS from OS isolates patients who are alive after progression.

Step 1 · build the partition
\[\begin{aligned} \mathrm{Progression\!\!\!\!-free}(t) &= \mathrm{PFS}(t) \\ \mathrm{Progressed\ and\ alive}(t) &= \mathrm{OS}(t)-\mathrm{PFS}(t) \\ \mathrm{Dead}(t) &= 1-\mathrm{OS}(t)\end{aligned}\]

These three proportions are mutually exclusive and sum to 100%, provided \(0\leq\mathrm{PFS}(t)\leq\mathrm{OS}(t)\leq1\).

Step 3 · structural dislinkage

What does structural dislinkage mean?

PFS and OS are each specified as separate survival functions. The model does not include an equation that forces better PFS to improve OS. Their common event data may be correlated, but the two fitted projections have no built-in clinical link. Change one and observe that the other can stay exactly fixed.

A smaller hazard ratio makes PFS decline more slowly, holding the comparator PFS constant.
OS is controlled separately. Changing PFS does not update this input.

The exponential curves in this teaching example

\[ S_{e,a}(t)=\exp\!\big[-\lambda_{e,\mathrm{control}}\,HR_{e,a}\,t\big] \]

Here \(e\) is PFS or OS, \(t\) is months, and each endpoint has its own baseline hazard \(\lambda\) and treatment hazard ratio \(HR\). The comparator has \(HR=1\). No patient-level correlation is implied by these formulas.

Step 4 · from area to economic outcomes

State area gives time. Time multiplied by utility gives QALYs.

Each thin vertical slice of a curve tells us the proportion in a health state. Summing those slices over time gives mean time spent in that state. Multiplying time by a state utility (quality-of-life weight) produces quality-adjusted life years (QALYs). Monthly costs are accumulated in the same way.

\[\begin{aligned}\mathrm{LY}_{\mathrm{PF}}&=\frac{1}{12}\int_0^H \mathrm{PFS}(t)\,dt\\\mathrm{LY}_{\mathrm{PD}}&=\frac{1}{12}\int_0^H [\mathrm{OS}(t)-\mathrm{PFS}(t)]\,dt\\ \mathrm{QALYs}&=\frac{1}{12}\int_0^H\big[u_{\mathrm{PF}}\mathrm{PFS}(t)+u_{\mathrm{PD}}(\mathrm{OS}(t)-\mathrm{PFS}(t))\big]D(t)\,dt\end{aligned}\]

Drug costs are fixed at an illustrative 1,800 per month (comparator) and 6,200 per month (treatment). In Part 1, treatment is assumed to continue while progression-free; Part 2 changes this exposure rule.

Show exactly how the calculations work

The program takes time steps of 0.25 months, evaluates the state probabilities at each step midpoint, multiplies by time, cost or utility weights, and sums. The discount factor is \(D(t)=(1+r)^{-t/12}\). Costs are in hypothetical currency units. For the basic model:

\[C=\int_0^H\left[c_{\mathrm{PF}}\mathrm{PFS}(t)+c_{\mathrm{PD}}(\mathrm{OS}(t)-\mathrm{PFS}(t))+c_{\mathrm{drug}}\mathrm{PFS}(t)\right]D(t)\,dt\]

The time step is numerical integration, not a health-state transition cycle. Cost and effect discounting use the same rate only for this demonstration. Undiscounted state life years are reported separately.

Part two · extra endpoints and exposure

Add TTP, time on treatment and duration of treatment, without confusing their roles.

Adding more curves does not automatically create more identifiable health states. First distinguish what event each curve measures. Then decide whether the information describes disease occupancy, treatment exposure, or an additional diagnostic about progression.

Step 3 · when can we create four categories?

What do PFS, OS and ToT tell us about treatment status?

If treatment definitely stops by progression, on-treatment patients are a subset of those who are progression-free. The three curves define four mutually exclusive groups:

\[\begin{aligned} \mathrm{PF,on}(t)&=\mathrm{ToT}(t)\\\mathrm{PF,off}(t)&=\mathrm{PFS}(t)-\mathrm{ToT}(t)\\\mathrm{Progressed,alive}(t)&=\mathrm{OS}(t)-\mathrm{PFS}(t)\\\mathrm{Dead}(t)&=1-\mathrm{OS}(t)\end{aligned}\]

But if patients can continue initial treatment after progression, ToT and PFS overlap without one being a subset of the other. Even valid marginal curves do not identify the proportion who are both progression-free and on treatment.

Step 4 · ToT curves versus DoT summaries

ToT tells us who is still treated. DoT tells us how long treatment lasts.

In this lab ToT is a survival-type curve, \(S_{\mathrm{ToT}}(t)=\Pr(T_{\mathrm{stop}}>t)\), for patients who start initial treatment at month 0. Permanent discontinuation or death ends treatment. DoT is a patient's duration from treatment start to permanent stopping. We can summarise the distribution of DoT using the mean, median or a restricted mean within the model horizon. These are two views of the same time-to-stop endpoint, not two independent pieces of information.

Why mean, median and treatment time differ

Median DoT: the time at which the ToT curve falls to 50%. It is not the average amount of treatment supplied.

Restricted mean DoT: the area under ToT up to a specified month. It measures expected treatment-exposure months per original patient over that horizon.

Actual drug consumption: depends additionally on dose, administration frequency, interruptions, dose reductions and packaging. Months on treatment alone cannot give the exact number of vials.

\[\begin{aligned}S_{\mathrm{ToT}}(t)&=\Pr(T_{\mathrm{stop}}>t)\\ \operatorname{RMDoT}(H)&=\int_0^H S_{\mathrm{ToT}}(t)\,dt\\ \text{Exponential example: }S_{\mathrm{ToT}}(t)&=e^{-\lambda_{\mathrm{stop}}t}\\ \operatorname{RMDoT}(H)&=\frac{1-e^{-\lambda_{\mathrm{stop}}H}}{\lambda_{\mathrm{stop}}},\quad \operatorname{MedianDoT}=\frac{\ln 2}{\lambda_{\mathrm{stop}}}\end{aligned}\]

For exponential curves, the unrestricted mean is \(1/\lambda_{\mathrm{stop}}\). The mean shown here is restricted to the model horizon; if the drug has a maximum authorised course, the exposure integral instead ends at the earlier treatment cap. A median may not be observed in immature trial follow-up, even when a parametric projection supplies one.

Do not average only completed courses. Patients still on treatment at data cut-off have censored durations. For a valid estimate, use an appropriate time-to-event method or individual exposure data. If a source reports a DoT mean or median without a curve, its distribution and tail are not identified without additional assumptions.
Step 5 · extra curves inform cost differently

Time on treatment determines drug exposure, not survival benefit

The economic model still uses PFS and OS to calculate PF and progressed health-state time. It uses the ToT curve to calculate initial treatment cost. Changing treatment discontinuation can change cost while leaving PFS, OS and QALYs unchanged.

\[C=\int_0^H\left[c_{\mathrm{PF}}\mathrm{PFS}(t)+c_{\mathrm{PD}}\big(\mathrm{OS}(t)-\mathrm{PFS}(t)\big)+c_{\mathrm{drug}}\mathrm{ToT}(t)\right]D(t)\,dt\]

These cost equations assume a constant monthly initial-treatment cost while on therapy. They do not model treatment starts, restarts, dose interruptions or subsequent-line treatment. They are for illustrating the role of exposure, not a complete oncology cost model.

Two worked checks

A. If \(S_{\mathrm{ToT}}(12)=0.40\), then about 40 of 100 original patients remain on initial therapy at month 12, assuming everyone started at baseline. This does not mean mean DoT equals 12 × 0.40 months; the mean requires the whole curve.

B. If the mean DoT is 16 months and median DoT is 11 months, pricing 11 months for every patient is incorrect. Use expected exposure, scheduled doses and the cost structure, with suitable handling of censoring.

  4. Clinical and real-world TTD operational definitions. Modelling safeguards: The worked exponential functions, hazard decomposition for TTP, ToT discontinuation rules, overlap bounds, DoT formulas and dosing-cost calculators are purpose-built teaching examples. They are not results reported in either cited source. Independent endpoint modelling does not mean the endpoints or event times are statistically independent in a patient-level trial dataset.

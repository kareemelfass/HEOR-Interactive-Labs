Chapter 0 · Before any equation: what are we trying to measure?

What you will understand: Why ordinary life-years are not enough, why different measures use different reference points, and what one numerical result actually tells you. The goal is to understand the measurement first, not memorise four abbreviations.

0.1. Start with a person, not with an abbreviation

Imagine someone diagnosed with a chronic disease at age 40. We follow them for the next ten years. We might want to know whether treatment keeps them alive, whether they feel and function better during the time they are alive, whether illness causes productive activity to be lost, or how much health is missing compared with an ideal life history. These are different questions. A single number cannot answer all of them unless we first decide what is being counted.

Start with the simplest unit: one life-year means one calendar year spent alive. If somebody survives for six years after entering a model, they contribute six life-years. This says nothing about whether they lived free of symptoms, required help with daily activities, were able to work, or died earlier than a reference lifespan.

Now imagine that the person survives for six of the ten years we are considering, has an average health value of 0.75 while alive, and has an assumed productivity index of 0.60 during those six years. We will use these same six years to explain the various ideas. All values are invented for education.

What changes between rows? The person's observed history does not change. We change the question, the weight, or the reference. A QALY weight does not become a disability weight merely because both use numbers between zero and one. Nor does a productivity index say how much somebody values their health.

0.2. Two directions of measurement: attained health and missing health

Attained health

Imagine drawing a rectangle for each year lived. Its width is one year, and its height is the health utility during that year. The total area is the amount of health attained. This is the basic QALY idea.

If four years are lived at utility 0.75, the total area is four rectangles, each 0.75 high: 3 QALYs.

What if health improves? Higher rectangle heights give more QALYs, even if life expectancy is unchanged. What if survival improves? More rectangles can give more QALYs, even if their heights stay the same.

Health missing from a reference

Instead, draw a rectangle representing a chosen ideal: ten years at full health, height 1. Now subtract the health actually attained. The missing area has two sources: impaired health during lived years, and the reference years never lived.

Here the missing area is 10 − 4.5 = 5.5. That equals 1.5 lost through impaired health and 4 lost through premature death under our illustrative reference.

The crucial choice: which reference lifespan and which health-loss weights are justified? Changing them changes what “lost” means.

The visual segments are weighted year-equivalents, not literal periods spent fully healthy or ill. The person lived six years throughout the blue and gold portions together. The last four calendar years represent the assumed reference gap after death.

0.3. The four measures, defined without circular wording

QALY

Quality-adjusted life-year

The question: After taking account of the time someone survives and how their health states are valued, how many full-health-equivalent years did they experience?

The arithmetic: For each time interval, multiply time alive by the health-state utility and add all intervals. A health utility is a preference-based value anchored at 0 for death and 1 for full health; certain states may have negative values.

Meaning: 3 QALYs might come from 3 years in full health or 4 years at utility 0.75. These histories are represented by the same amount under the conventional multiplicative QALY rule, but they are not literally the same lived experience.

HEOR direction: Compare treatment with its alternative. More QALYs gained is a positive health effect.

DALY

Disability-adjusted life-year

The question: How many years of healthy life were lost due to premature death and time lived with disability, relative to a reference?

The arithmetic: Add years of life lost due to premature death (YLL) and years lived with disability, weighted by severity (YLD).

Meaning: A disease generating 100 DALYs has produced the equivalent of 100 healthy years of burden across the specified population, not necessarily 100 deaths or 100 years in which people were disabled.

HEOR direction: More DALY burden is worse. More DALYs averted by an intervention is better.

HALY

Health-adjusted life-years, a family, not one universal formula

The question: How can we construct a measure of population health that includes achieved health, missing health, or both, and what social judgments are hidden in that choice?

The arithmetic: The term HALY is used broadly in applied studies. The specific theoretical family in Moreno-Ternero et al. (2023) uses achieved quality-weighted lifetime minus a parameter-weighted reference lifetime.

Meaning: Different members of the theoretical family evaluate the same population from an attainment perspective or a reference-gap perspective. It would be misleading to describe every reported “HALY gained” in published studies as an application of the paper's parameter.

HEOR direction: Ask which exact HALY definition the study used. Chapter 3 derives the theoretical family from the first principles above.

PALY

Productivity-adjusted life-year

The question: Over time alive and within a defined productive period, how much productive activity was performed relative to full productivity under the chosen convention?

The arithmetic: Multiply lived time by a productivity index. That index may reflect missed work, reduced performance, labour-force participation and workforce exit.

Meaning: Five years at productivity index 0.6 contribute 3 PALYs. This does not mean only three years were lived, that the person's health was rated 0.6, or that the other two years had no social value.

HEOR direction: PALYs help analyse productivity burden, most commonly as a societal or economic consequence, not as a replacement for QALYs.

0.4. Four terms that must not be used interchangeably

Life expectancy: the expected additional or total life-time in a population under a specified survival pattern. It is not a utility and is not always the same as the benchmark used for DALYs. For a person dying at a particular age, GBD YLL uses a standard remaining life expectancy at that age.

Health utility: a number expressing the valuation of a described health state, obtained using a preference-based approach. A utility of 0.5 is not a 50% probability of survival.

Disability weight: a severity weight for health loss due to disability. A value of 0.5 is not the proportion of patients disabled. Published GBD disability weights are not generally equal to one minus a QALY utility because the two measures are constructed and elicited differently. [R1]

Productivity index: a fraction representing activity or output under a particular definition. A 0.5 index is not a person's quality of life. If the index includes labour participation, make sure participation has not already reduced the population being modelled. [R3, R4]

The main interpretive rule: Always state the comparator, population, horizon, reference lifespan, source of weights and whether the outcome is an amount attained, an amount lost, or a difference between strategies. A numerical result without those elements has no unambiguous interpretation.

1. QALY · Build health-adjusted time

Learning message: Each interval contributes its duration multiplied by health utility. The total is the sum of those contributions.

Start with the concept · What is a QALY?

A quality-adjusted life-year (QALY) combines how long someone lives with how their health during those years is valued. One year at full health (utility 1) yields 1 QALY. A year at utility 0.5 yields 0.5 QALYs, even though the person lives for the full year.

Calculation: Multiply time lived in each health state by its preference-based utility, then add the intervals. Interpretation: More QALYs means more total preferred health over the specified horizon. An intervention's QALY gain is the difference between intervention and comparator totals.

\[QALYs = \sum_j \Delta t_j\,u_j, \qquad \Delta Q = Q_T-Q_C\]
Worked example: Year 1 at utility 0.90 and year 2 at utility 0.60: \(1(0.90)+1(0.60)=1.50\) QALYs. Two calendar years do not imply two QALYs. If another treatment gives 1.70 QALYs, its incremental benefit is \(1.70-1.50=0.20\) QALYs.

The next calculator assumes a constant utility over the chosen duration. For changing health states, break the timeline into intervals; for time-varying observed utility, use a justified area-under-the-utility-curve method.

Change the health trajectory

Interpretation: A negative QALY utility is possible for states valued worse than death. This is not a negative probability. For a constant state, utility is applied to the entire time lived in that state.

Rebuild the arithmetic

With variable health: split follow-up into intervals and sum each interval's \(\Delta t\times u\). Where utility is measured at successive times, a trapezoidal area approximation may be used, subject to the measurement assumptions.

Why does a 0.08 utility improvement create QALYs even if survival does not change?

For 100 people alive throughout one year, moving utility from 0.70 to 0.78 creates \(100(0.78-0.70)=8\) additional QALYs without adding one life-year. A QALY benefit need not be smaller than the incremental life-year benefit.

Where should utilities come from?

Use preference-based weights from appropriate instruments and value sets, such as EQ-5D. Patient-reported symptom scales and raw visual analogue scores are not automatically anchored utilities. Country-specific values, age norms, and states worse than death require explicit consideration. [R1]

Use in HEOR · Published Egyptian pneumococcal-vaccination model

Published cost-utility analysis

Sevilla et al. (2022) compared introducing PCV13 or PCV10 childhood vaccination programmes with no vaccination in Egypt, and also compared PCV13 with PCV10. They projected outcomes across 100 successive birth cohorts, with a disease incidence and multiple-cohort model. QALY gains were used with incremental costs to calculate the ICER.

Reported base-case results, in 2016 US$: PCV13 versus no programme: $926 per QALY gained. PCV10 versus no programme: $1,984 per QALY. PCV13 versus PCV10: $174 per QALY. In the published per-vaccinated-infant illustration, PCV13 versus no programme yielded 0.0462 incremental QALYs at $42.75 incremental cost. \(42.75/0.0462\approx925.3\); the paper reports $925.60 from underlying unrounded values. [R5]

Why QALYs here? The vaccine can prevent both deaths and nonfatal disease, so combining morbidity and mortality allows a single cost-utility comparison. The model's horizon, discounting, assumed efficacy and herd effects determine the result; these are not clinical trial observations for an individual child.

Read the published study

Caveats and common pitfalls · QALY

  • Do not confuse utility with survival: utility weights health while alive. Survival probabilities determine time spent alive.
  • Use preference-based values: a symptom score or a raw health questionnaire total is not automatically a QALY utility.
  • Check the valuation population: EQ-5D value sets, age norms and whose preferences are used may change QALYs.
  • Handle negative values: some health states are valued worse than death; restricting all utilities to 0–1 may be inappropriate.
  • Match timing: short adverse events need their actual duration, not a whole-year decrement.
  • Do not mix conventions: comparator, model horizon, cycle correction and discounting must be consistent between arms.
  • Interpret the gain, not just the total: 7 QALYs in the treated arm does not mean 7 QALYs gained by treatment.

Methodological context: Turner et al. [R1].

2. DALY · Separate premature death from disability

Learning message: Conventional DALY burden combines YLL (life-years lost against a reference life table) and YLD (years lived with disability).

Start with the concept · What is a DALY?

A disability-adjusted life-year (DALY) is a year of full health lost. Unlike QALYs, which generally measure health attained, DALYs count loss. Two things cause loss: premature death contributes years of life lost (YLL), while living with illness contributes years lived with disability (YLD).

\[DALY=YLL+YLD,\qquad YLL=D\times e^*(a),\qquad YLD\approx \text{duration}\times\text{disability weight}\]

Meaning: More DALYs indicate more burden. A treatment is beneficial when it averts DALYs: \(DALY_{comparator}-DALY_{treatment}\).

Worked example: One death causes 23 YLL when the assumed remaining reference expectancy at death is 23 years. Four separate illness episodes lasting 0.5 years each at disability weight 0.25 contribute \(4\times0.5\times0.25=0.5\) YLD. Total = 23.5 DALYs. The reference lifespan and disability weight are illustrative, not GBD inputs.

YLL and YLD do not have to arise in the same person. The DALY burden from a population is aggregated across people and relevant disease sequelae.

A. Mortality · Years of life lost (YLL)

\[\mathrm{YLL}=D(a)\times e^*(a)\]

Use remaining life expectancy conditional on reaching the age at death. Do not subtract age at death from life expectancy at birth. In a real GBD analysis, obtain an actual standard reference life table.

B. Morbidity · Years lived with disability (YLD)

For incidence, this represents duration-weighted cases with a single episode. For prevalence, multiply prevalent cases during the year by disability weight, under the stated person-year convention. These are not interchangeable attribution methods.

C. Add them, then compare strategies

\[\mathrm{DALY}=\mathrm{YLL}+\mathrm{YLD}\qquad \mathrm{DALYs\ averted}=B_C-B_T\]

Suppose an intervention reduces DALY burden from 250 to 190. It averts \(250-190=60\) DALYs. It does not avert 190 DALYs.

GBD versus economic evaluation: Current GBD burden estimates do not apply historical age weighting or time discounting. Economic evaluations may discount DALYs averted according to their appraisal guidelines. This is a change in use and methodology, not a contradiction. [R1]

Use in HEOR · Published Egyptian PCV13 economic analysis

Published disease-burden economic evaluation

Sibak et al. (2015) compared introducing PCV13 into Egypt's national immunisation programme with no vaccination, modelling ten childhood birth cohorts in the TRIVAC model from a government perspective.

Reported projections: The programme was estimated to avert 8,583 pneumococcal deaths and 272,878 DALYs across ten cohorts, with an ICER of US$3,916 per DALY averted. [R6]

Why DALYs here? Prevention avoids both severe nonfatal infections and premature deaths, while the DALY burden gives one comparable health-loss denominator for resource allocation.

Important contrast: This 2015 DALY model and the 2022 QALY model in the previous chapter used different designs and cohorts. Their reported cost-per-outcome ratios cannot be interpreted as a head-to-head comparison of QALYs versus DALYs.

Read the published study

Caveats and common pitfalls · DALY

  • Never use life expectancy at birth minus age at death: obtain reference remaining life expectancy at that age.
  • Distinguish incidence from prevalence: future morbidity attributed to incident cases and morbidity occurring in a calendar year answer different questions.
  • Do not automatically set disability weight = 1 − utility: GBD disability weights and QALY utilities are elicited differently.
  • Respect episode duration: 30 days of disability do not count as 365 days.
  • Do not mistake burden for benefit: an intervention averts the difference in DALY burden.
  • Do not import historical GBD conventions: current GBD burden estimates exclude older age-weighting and health discounting; economic evaluations may follow different discounting rules.
  • Check overlapping disease states: comorbidities may require a justified combined disability-weight rule.

Methodological context: Turner et al. [R1].

3. HALY · Explore a family of population health scores

Learning message: In the supplied 2023 framework, the parameter \(\alpha\) controls the importance of reference lifetime. It is a mathematical family, not another standard standalone HEOR endpoint.

Start with the concept · What does HALY mean?

Health-adjusted life-years (HALYs) is a broad label for life-years adjusted for health. It is not always one uniquely specified endpoint. In the supplied Moreno-Ternero et al. (2023) paper, the important contribution is a theoretical family of population health evaluation functions, meaning mathematical rules that assign one overall score to a population's health profile.

That family combines attained quality-adjusted lifetime with a penalty for reference lifetime. A reference lifetime is an explicitly defined benchmark for how long life might ideally extend. The parameter \(\alpha\) determines how strongly that benchmark enters the social score.

\[P_{\alpha}=\sum_i(q_it_i-\alpha r_i)\]
Worked example: One person lives 4 years at health weight 0.8 with a reference of 5 years. Attained health: \(4\times0.8=3.2\). At \(\alpha=0.4\), score = \(3.2-0.4\times5=1.2\). At \(\alpha=0\), the score is 3.2 QALY-like attained years. At \(\alpha=1\), it is \(3.2-5=-1.8\): the negative shortfall score, not a positive DALY count.

Meaning: the parameter reveals which normative reference matters, and whether different population comparisons change when that reference changes. The paper's bounded-health-weight setting does not automatically cover negative QALY utilities.

\[P_\alpha=\sum_{i=1}^n \left(q_i t_i-\alpha r_i\right),\qquad 0\leq\alpha\leq 1\]

Use in HEOR · Published health-adjusted life-year modelling

Published population modelling

Marklund et al. (2024) evaluated mandatory sodium limits in Australia's packaged foods. A multi-cohort, multistate life-table model compared mandatory WHO benchmarks with the existing status quo, translating sodium reduction into blood-pressure, cardiovascular disease and survival outcomes.

Reported 10-year projection: 11,174 HALYs gained, 2,743 cardiovascular deaths averted and AUD$223 million of healthcare cost savings for mandatory WHO benchmarks compared with status quo. Costs and HALYs were discounted at 3%. [R7]

How HALYs were useful: A single adjusted health outcome allowed a national prevention strategy's morbidity and mortality effects to be combined for an economic evaluation.

Essential distinction: The paper uses HALYs as a practical health-adjusted outcome. It does not establish an applied estimation of the Moreno-Ternero \(\alpha\)-parameter family. The interactive slider teaches that theoretical family, not a calibration to this empirical paper.

Read the published study

Caveats and common pitfalls · HALY

  • Define HALY operationally: a study saying “HALYs gained” may use a conventional QALY-like or another health-adjusted construction, not the 2023 \(\alpha\) family.
  • Interpret the sign: the paper's DALY pole is a negative shortfall population score, whereas conventional DALYs are a positive burden count.
  • Reference lifetimes are a normative choice: changing them can change results, so use and justify a common reference when appropriate.
  • Do not interpret \(\alpha\) as an estimated treatment effect: it is a weight expressing how references enter the evaluation.
  • Bounded health weights matter: assumptions behind the published theorems do not automatically accommodate negative utility states.
  • Identical references cancel in increments: apparent α-sensitivity should be checked for changes in the reference convention rather than claimed as a clinical benefit.

Methodological context: Moreno-Ternero et al. [R2].

4. PALY · Construct the productivity index

Learning message: Paid-work productivity combines how much work is performed, how effective work is, and, if included, workforce participation. It is not a health utility.

Start with the concept · What is a PALY?

A productivity-adjusted life-year (PALY) combines how many years a person is alive during the relevant productive period with how much of their potential productive activity is realised. Productivity loss may arise from absenteeism (missing work), presenteeism (reduced output while present), early workforce exit or changes in workforce participation.

\[PALYs=\sum_j \text{years lived}_j\times\text{productivity index}_j\]

Meaning: a PALY is a full-productivity-equivalent year under the chosen productivity definition. It does not measure quality of life and does not imply that someone who is not in paid employment has no worth or no social contribution.

Worked example: With 240 nominal annual workdays, 24 days missed, and 20% impairment when present, the productive fraction while participating is \((240-24)/240\times(1-0.20)=0.90\times0.80=0.72\). If the analysis also appropriately applies 85% participation, the combined index becomes \(0.72\times0.85=0.612\), yielding 3.06 PALYs over five years at the same index.

The product here is a clearly specified illustration. Actual PALY studies vary in the use of employment, absenteeism, presenteeism, mortality and the value of unpaid work.

Not a verified payer saving or a causal GDP estimate.

This example adopts a multiplicative combination of absence, impairment when present and participation to keep denominator definitions explicit. Other published models construct the index differently. The participation factor must match the target population and must not be applied twice.

Avoid double counting: PALYs usually capture paid work and are often monetised using GDP per full-time worker or wage-based methods. If productivity gains also enter the cost side, do not add their monetary value again as independent benefits. Ademi et al. (2021) specifically caution against treating cost per PALY as a routine ratio because of PALYs' inherent monetary valuation. [R3–R4]

Use in HEOR · Published hypertension productivity burden

Published life-table productivity model

Hird et al. (2019) modelled working-age Australian adults with hypertension (aged 20–69 years) using life tables and simulated follow-up through working age. The analysis combined the consequences of premature death and reduced work productivity.

Reported projections: Hypertension was associated with 609,801 PALYs lost across the simulated working lifetimes. Adequate treatment and control for everyone with hypertension was projected to preserve 342,538 PALYs, valued at AUD$76.4 billion in GDP output retained. [R8]

Why PALYs here? The outcome expresses the productivity burden over the working lifetime, beyond health-related quality of life. The monetary value describes estimated economic output, not a verified profit gain or direct healthcare saving.

Read the published study

Caveats and common pitfalls · PALY

  • Paid work is not all productive activity: unpaid caregiving and informal work may be omitted, creating distributional bias.
  • Specify working-age limits: models using different upper and lower age limits may have systematically different PALYs.
  • Prevent double adjustments: do not apply workforce participation twice or reduce both life-years and a separate productivity weight for the same death.
  • Match denominators: absence as a fraction of scheduled working days and presenteeism among days present cannot simply be added without checking their definitions.
  • GDP is not individual profit: GDP-per-worker and wage-based valuations estimate different things; neither automatically represents cash saved by the payer.
  • Avoid double counting costs: do not add monetised PALYs to an ICER already accounting for the same lost productivity.
  • Do not use PALYs to rank people's worth: low observed productivity does not mean a life has low health value.

Methodological context: Ademi et al. [R3, R4].

5. One intervention, four perspectives · Full cohort calculator

Learning message: Use the same survival cohort for all outcomes. First compute exposure to life, then apply utility, disability or productivity weights. Keep DALY reference conventions explicit.

What this chapter calculates and why

The integrated cohort reuses one set of annual survival counts while assigning health, disability and productivity weights to the same person-time. We first estimate life-years from the number alive in each cycle; then calculate QALYs and PALYs by multiplying time alive by utility or productivity index. We calculate two explicitly different health-loss constructions: a within-five-year shortfall and a reference-based YLL-plus-YLD burden. The result shows how a single intervention can deliver different-looking outcomes without implying contradiction.

HEOR use: A similar structure underlies economic models combining time in health states, utilities, mortality and costs. The published pneumococcal, sodium-reduction and hypertension studies above illustrate why choosing the decision question and reference method matters. Their actual model structures and horizons differ from this teaching cohort.

Pitfall: Never compare a five-year QALY gain directly with a DALY burden whose mortality term includes decades beyond year five.

Base case from the supplied Markdown tutorial: 100 adults aged 40, five one-year cycles. With the default treatment survival and weight improvements, results reproduce +30 life-years, +57.45 QALYs, +65.625 PALYs and 526.45 conventional-reference illustrative DALYs averted per 100 starting adults, all undiscounted. The latter includes YLL beyond the five-year horizon and is not directly comparable with the five-year QALYs gained.

6. What changes the answer? · Comparison and decision process

The measure must follow the decision question, the valuation framework and the available evidence.

How to select a measure

First: Is the objective health attainment in an intervention appraisal, the burden of disease relative to a normative reference, or productive output lost?

Second: What population and horizon are relevant? Explicitly state whether you follow living patients, count deaths and lost lifetime from a standard reference, or consider only working years.

Third: Which valid weights and reference data exist, and what do the applicable HTA or public-health guidelines require?

Finally: Audit the denominator, sign, comparator, discounting and potential double counting before interpreting an incremental ratio.

Testable equivalence condition

\[B=R-Q,\qquad \Delta B_{\mathrm{averted}}=B_C-B_T=Q_T-Q_C\]

This identity requires a common reference \(R\) and a consistent complement convention for the weight. It does not generally hold for reported GBD DALYs because those may use different disability weights, reference lifetimes and attribution horizons.

PALYs are not QALYs: Better productivity is not evidence of equivalent health preference gains, particularly for unpaid work, retired people, informal employment and work outside the formal labour market.

  4. [R8] Hird TR et al. (2019). Productivity Burden of Hypertension in Australia. Hypertension 73:777–784. DOI. Working-lifetime PALYs and the GDP consequences of hypertension control.

What is constructed? The 100-person five-year cohort, survival counts, utilities, productivity indices, reference years and prices are invented for teaching. The weight equality \(d=1-u\) is used only where expressly noted to show an algebraic identity. No invented values are attributed to the four papers.

Methodological boundaries: This is not a validated GBD burden-of-disease engine, a jurisdiction-specific submission model or a real-world evidence source. The HALY family is based on bounded nonnegative quality weights, whereas the separate QALY calculator permits negative utilities. A direct substitution of a negative utility in the bounded HALY theorem is not justified.

Technical: Static HTML, CSS and JavaScript with MathJax loaded from a public CDN. Calculators, tables, quiz and SVG visualisations run locally in-browser without server, login or analytics. Mathematical notation falls back to its source text if the CDN is unavailable.
